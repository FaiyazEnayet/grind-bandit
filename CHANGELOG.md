# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Multi-topic credit-assignment sensitivity audit (`src/grindbandit/credit_audit.py`, `tests/test_credit_audit.py`). Live mastery credits every tagged topic equally, but 190 of the 218 curated problems carry multiple tags, so the choice of credit rule moves the mastery picture. The audit replays the same logged attempts offline under three rules and reports the per-topic differences: full credit (the live rule), fractional credit split evenly across the tags, and credit only to the user-reported blocked topic, with a counted fallback to fractional when an attempt names no blocked topic. The audit builds its own fresh state, never mutates the entries it is given, and never feeds the bandit or the reward, and its results are labeled counterfactual modeling of one log under different assumptions, not evidence that any rule learns better; a blocked-topic report is a subjective annotation used as a modeling input, never treated as ground truth. The full-credit replay matches production `mastery.replay` exactly, including decay across gaps, and the gate pins exact known-answer mastery values for single- and multi-topic solves and failures, stable replay, rejection of invalid tag input, and no mutation of the original log. Run the gate with `python -m pytest -q -k stage3_grind_bandit_11`.
- Transfer-aware problem families and novelty controls (`src/grindbandit/data/problem_families.json`, `src/grindbandit/problems.py`, `src/grindbandit/state.py`, `src/grindbandit/research/transfer_splits.py`, `tests/test_transfer_families.py`, `docs/model.md`). The delayed quiz already picked a different problem on the same topic, but a different problem ID does not prove novel transfer when both problems share a solution template. The bank now carries a curated, versioned family layer (`families-v1`): 29 solution-template families, for example BFS flood fill versus BFS shortest path and binary search on the answer versus binary search on an index, with explicit multi-label tags on all 218 problems, human-reviewed, and a note wherever two templates are both canonical for a problem. The quiz chooser still ranks novelty first, then prefers a different solution-template family where one is available, and every quiz record carries a transfer block naming the tag (near-transfer, same-family, repeat, or unknown), whether family novelty is satisfied, both family sets, and whether any family-different alternative existed in the pool. A quiz counts as novel transfer only when it is both unseen and near-transfer, so a repeat is never counted as novel transfer. The research module splits the bank deterministically by problem or by whole primary families (seed 7 holds out 9 families, 62 of 218 problems) and audits family leakage across the split: secondary tags still co-occur across a family-held-out split and the audit reports that, along with the blocked cases (the one-problem prefix-sums family, and the intervals topic, whose problems all share the greedy-interval family). Worked example: a quiz sourced from Two Sum (hash-map counting) on the array topic picks Median of Two Sorted Arrays (binary search on the answer), tagged near-transfer with family novelty satisfied. Run the gate with `python -m pytest -q -k stage3_grind_bandit_16`.
- Budget-aware recommendation objectives and policy alternatives, shadow-only (`src/grindbandit/policies/registry.py`, `src/grindbandit/policies/time_aware.py`, `src/grindbandit/research/policy_comparison.py`, `src/grindbandit/research/evaluate_policies.py`, `docs/model.md`). A versioned policy branch (`policy-v1`) records current LinTS as the sole production policy and five challengers as shadow: shortest expected-duration, weakest-topic, due-first spaced schedule, contextual cost-aware LinTS, and constrained LinTS that filters out problems exceeding the remaining budget and picks nothing when nothing fits. Candidate features are the user time budget, historically observed minutes (leave-one-problem-out by difficulty, so a problem never predicts its own duration, with predeclared priors of 15, 30, and 45 minutes per difficulty when a difficulty has no history), difficulty, novelty, and interval since the target skill was practiced. Utility is U = predicted delayed-transfer benefit minus lambda times expected time (lambda 0.01 reward units per minute), explicitly hypothetical until a valid predictive head exists, since the benefit is the sampled LinTS score in model-derived reward units rather than measured transfer. All six policies draw from the same eligible set under the live eligibility rule, and the comparison runs them through fixed 15, 30, and 60 minute simulation sessions with a known ground truth (true mastery strong on array and string, weak on dp, graph, and backtracking; true durations around 12, 25, and 45 minutes by difficulty; match-gated learning; transfer as mean solve probability on novel quiz problems fixed before the session). The gate adopts a challenger only when its transfer improvement over LinTS has a paired bootstrap 95 percent interval entirely above zero, its mean overrun is at most 5 percent of the budget, and it does not starve Hard topics or narrow topic coverage below a quarter and a half of LinTS respectively. The measured result is an honest null: at 30 replications, no challenger beats LinTS on transfer at any budget (weakest-topic is closest at -0.0006, -0.0006, -0.0010; shortest-duration falls furthest at 60 minutes, -0.0060), the same no-winner verdict holds at a second seed, and the machinery still separates the policies where it should (LinTS mean overrun 17.17, 14.21, and 17.09 minutes against 0.69, 1.72, and 1.32 for constrained LinTS, while shortest-duration and due-first pick no Hard problems at 15 minutes and trip the starvation flag). On the scripted demo history the real-data verdict is NOT IDENTIFIABLE (0 linked delayed-transfer observations against the 30 minimum), so the production mastery model, bandit, and reward are untouched, no live-selection integration was added, and no feature version was bumped. Run it with `python -m grindbandit.research.evaluate_policies --state PATH` (or `--demo` for the scripted history, plus `--simulate` for the comparison).
- Shadow skill model separating ability from difficulty (`src/grindbandit/research/skill_model.py`, `src/grindbandit/research/calibration.py`, `src/grindbandit/research/evaluate_skill.py`, `docs/model.md`). Untouched topics default to 0.5 modeled mastery and the difficulty fit uses hard thresholds, while the bank holds only 10 Hard problems, so a failed Hard problem may reflect difficulty as much as lack of topic competence. The shadow candidates predict next-attempt correctness from attempt records carrying problem id, difficulty, topics, and outcome, fitted on training history only: a hierarchical Beta-binomial with a fixed shrinkage strength of 8 pseudo-observations toward the global rate, and a small regularized Rasch-style model (one ability per topic, shrunk toward a global ability, plus per-difficulty offsets with Medium anchored at zero). They are compared against the global solve rate, the existing per-topic means, and a deterministic per-difficulty rate heuristic on a predeclared temporal holdout (latest quarter) using Brier score, log loss, calibration bins, and per-topic and by-difficulty breakdowns. The gate adopts a learned model only when it beats all three baselines on holdout Brier and log loss, with a paired bootstrap 95 percent interval for its Brier improvement over the production topic means excluding zero and no pathological flag (a Rasch parameter on its bound, a failed fit, or a training set with no outcome variation). The production mastery model, bandit, and reward are untouched. On controlled simulation with a known truth the machinery behaves as designed: with both topic ability and difficulty in play (2,000 attempts, seed 7) the Rasch-style head is adopted (holdout Brier 0.1805 versus 0.2012 topic means, 0.2128 global, 0.1867 difficulty heuristic; log loss 0.5408 versus 0.5908, 0.6171, 0.5567; bootstrap interval -0.0291 to -0.0121), and the adoption is stable across seeds 7, 11, and 13. The fit recovers the truth approximately (ability -0.10 against 0, Easy -1.01 against -1.0, array 1.30 against 1.2) but splits the Hard/dp effect between the two (Hard 0.71 against 1.0, dp -0.44 against -1.2), because half of the 10 Hard problems are dp. With only topic signal, only difficulty signal, or no signal at all the gate correctly keeps the simple model. On the scripted demo history the verdict is NOT IDENTIFIABLE (30 observations against the 60 train plus 20 holdout minimum), so the fixed model stays and no real skill measurement has been fitted yet. Run it with `python -m grindbandit.research.evaluate_skill --state PATH` (or `--demo` for the scripted history).
- Shadow retention model with a learnable half-life (`src/grindbandit/research/retention.py`, `src/grindbandit/research/evaluate_retention.py`, `docs/model.md`). The production mastery decay is untouched at a fixed 30 days. The shadow candidates (a global learned half-life, a per-topic half-life shrunk toward the global value, and a monotone recurrence model where half-life grows with prior practice) predict recall as p = 2^(-D/h), are fitted on training periods only, and are compared on a predeclared temporal holdout (latest quarter) using Brier score, log loss, calibration bins, and due-date error. The gate adopts a learned model only when it beats both the trivial global rate and the fixed 30-day baseline on holdout Brier and log loss, with a paired bootstrap 95 percent interval for the Brier improvement excluding zero and no fitted half-life on the 1 to 365 day bounds; otherwise the simple model stays. On controlled simulation with a known truth the machinery behaves as designed: a true 12-day half-life is recovered at 10.68 days and wins on the holdout (Brier 0.1012 versus 0.1369 fixed, interval -0.0675 to -0.0038), while a true 30-day half-life is correctly rejected (Brier 0.0958 versus 0.0961 fixed, interval -0.0021 to 0.0014). On the scripted demo history the verdict is NOT IDENTIFIABLE (40 observations against the 60 train plus 20 holdout minimum), so the fixed model stays and no real retention measurement has been fitted yet. Run it with `python -m grindbandit.research.evaluate_retention --state PATH` (or `--demo` for the scripted history).
- Proxy reward alignment laboratory (`src/grindbandit/research/proxy_alignment.py`, `tests/test_proxy_alignment.py`). The bandit optimizes a model-derived reward (change in modeled mastery of the weakest topic), and this module checks offline whether ranking by that reward predicts delayed transfer: it joins each attempt to its delayed transfer quiz by the immutable `attempt_id`, preserving the realized delay, whether the quiz item was novel, how much practice exposure the quiz problem already had, and which outcomes are still missing. It reports rank association (Spearman) between the reward and the delayed outcome, discrimination (AUC) of the reward as a predictor of delayed success, calibration by reward tercile, and stratified failure maps by delay timing, item novelty, practice exposure, and topic. Missing or late outcomes are reported as sample attrition with best/worst case sensitivity bounds instead of being counted as failures, and no causal claim is made: quiz outcomes stay observational and never feed mastery, the posterior, or the reward. The analysis runs only with at least 30 completed linked observations and at least 10 in a predeclared, time-ordered holdout (the latest quarter by attempt time); below that the verdict is NOT IDENTIFIABLE, which is the current finding on the data available here: the scripted demo history holds 30 attempts and no quizzes at all, so it yields 0 linked observations. The diagnostics are validated against a controlled simulation with a known ground truth instead: in an aligned world (400 linked rows, seed 7) Spearman is 0.437 and AUC 0.753 with a holdout Spearman of 0.356, while in a misaligned world the same seed gives Spearman -0.013 and AUC 0.493. Simulated rows are labeled as such and are never presented as real practice data.
- Selection-probability estimator oracle and invariance (`src/grindbandit/bandit.py`, `tests/test_probability_estimation.py`). For two fixed candidate contexts the exact pick probability under the Gaussian posterior is now computed in closed form (`bandit.analytic_pair_selection_probability`): Phi(mu^T d / sqrt(d^T Sigma d)) for strictly positive variance, with degenerate cases (identical contexts, zero variance, exact ties) returning stable values instead of failing. The Monte Carlo estimator gains a companion (`bandit.estimate_selection_probabilities_with_error`) that returns the same add-one smoothed probabilities alongside a separate binomial simulation standard error per candidate, so simulation error is never confused with the estimate itself; the plain estimator now delegates to it and returns identical values. Regression tests pin the Monte Carlo estimate against the oracle within a tolerance justified from the standard error plus the smoothing shift (oracle 0.71477, estimate 0.71103 at 20,000 samples, difference 0.00374 against a 0.01608 allowance), pin exact known answers for the oracle, pin that mocking or bypassing the estimator and changing the sample count never change the selected problem, pin that estimation uses its own RNG stream, and pin that old recommendation records still replay to the same pick.
- Versioned state schema with a persisted migration path (`src/grindbandit/state.py`, `src/grindbandit/cli.py`, `docs/state-schema.md`). State files now record `schema_version` (current: 1); files written before versions existed count as version 0. Loads still migrate in memory only and never touch the file, but `grind-bandit migrate [--path PATH]` writes the migrated state back after copying the exact original bytes to `<path>.v<from>.bak` (written once; an existing backup is left alone so the earliest pre-migration copy is kept). Migrating a current file is a no-op; a file from a newer version is refused rather than silently downgraded. Migration invents no history: old log entries gain no attempt id or linkage, and no recommendations or quiz records are fabricated. The old-format example state at `tests/fixtures/state-v0-example.json` (three scripted attempts on real bank problems, values computed by the engine) migrates deterministically under these rules, and the demo-data builder's version 0 shaped state migrates the same way.
- Model documentation with worked numerical examples (`docs/model.md`). Mastery (Beta-Bernoulli with 30-day forgetting), the model-derived reward, the five context features, the Thompson sampling posterior update, eligibility and spaced review, and delayed quizzes are each documented with exact numbers reproduced by regression tests: ten solves then ninety idle days gives mastery 0.6923 and a failure right after gives 0.5294; a solve on a fresh topic rewards +1/6 and a failure -1/6; the fresh context for problem 28 is [0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 1.0, 1.0]; one observation moves the posterior mean to [0.02924, 0.02924, 0.02924, 0.05848, 0.05848]; seed 7 draws theta [0.001230, 0.298746, -0.274138, -0.890592, -0.454671].
- Precise attempt/recommendation lineage (`src/grindbandit/state.py`, `src/grindbandit/cli.py`). Every logged attempt now carries its own immutable `attempt_id`, and delayed quiz records name that exact attempt as `source_attempt_id`, so two attempts on the same problem never alias one source. The `log` command accepts `--recommendation-id`: the id is validated before anything is written and rejected when it is unknown, selected a different problem, is stale (a newer recommendation exists), or was already taken up by an earlier attempt. Without the flag, a link is only inferred from the globally latest recommendation when it is recent (within a day), unconsumed, and selected the logged problem, and the entry records it as `linkage_method: inferred`; explicit links record `linkage_method: explicit`, and a manually chosen attempt stays unlinked. Linked entries also carry a `recommendation_status` (exposed / accepted) where known. Nothing is fabricated for historical entries: state files written before this change load unchanged, their attempts gain no id or linkage retroactively, and their quizzes gain no source attempt id. Linkage metadata is bookkeeping only and never feeds mastery, the bandit posterior, or the model-derived reward; the demo-data builder passes deterministic attempt ids so its seeded history stays reproducible.
- Delayed quiz timing and assignment records (`src/grindbandit/state.py`, `src/grindbandit/cli.py`). Each quiz record now carries an `assignment` block: whether the problem was novel (never attempted, never assigned as a quiz), how many times it had been assigned before, and whether the topic's unused pool was exhausted, in which case the schedule message and the record both state that the assignment is a reuse. Each recorded outcome now stores the actual delay in seconds and an early / on-time / late status next to the solved flag and timestamp, and `stats` lists recorded outcomes with their timing alongside the pending quizzes (outcomes recorded before this change, which have no timing, are reported as such).
- Explicit eligibility policy and spaced-review resurfacing (`src/grindbandit/cli.py`, `src/grindbandit/bandit.py`, `scripts/common.py`). Candidate selection used to drop every ever-solved problem permanently, and when nothing remained it silently fell back to the full bank and reported that the user had solved every problem in the bank, which was false whenever unsolved problems were simply inside the one-day attempt cooldown. `cli.assess_eligibility` now classifies every problem in the bank with a deterministic reason: new_practice or due_review when eligible, cooldown_new_practice or review_cooldown when not. A solved problem resurfaces as a due review once the review interval (state config `review_interval_days`, default 7 days) has elapsed since its last attempt; the interval is a configurable starting heuristic, not a validated optimum. When no problem is eligible, `next` prints an explicit nothing-eligible message and appends no recommendation record instead of fabricating a pick. Recommendation records now carry a `policy` block with the review interval, the attempt cooldown, the feature version, and the eligibility entry for every problem in the bank, and each candidate carries its eligibility class and reason, so the logged eligible set can be checked against the policy. The demo-data helper in `scripts/common.py` follows the same policy and returns no pick when nothing is eligible.
- Context feature version `context-v2` (`src/grindbandit/bandit.py`). The recency feature now measures days since the least recently practiced topic on the problem, with never-practiced topics counting as the full 30-day cap: an untouched topic is the stalest signal a problem can carry. The context-v1 layout (most recent practice across topics, unseen topics ignored) is preserved as `bandit.context_v1`, and `bandit.replay_selection` dispatches v1 records to the preserved `replay_selection_v1` path, so records logged under either version replay to their logged picks.

### Fixed
- State saves are now atomic, validated, and serialized (`src/grindbandit/state.py`). A save writes to a temporary file in the same directory and moves it over the old file, so an interrupted serialization leaves the prior file readable instead of a partial file. A save takes an exclusive lock on a `<path>.lock` file first and fails with an explicit conflict when another process holds it, instead of silently overwriting that process's work. Loads validate the state shape and reject a wrongly shaped file with a named error; state files written before a top-level key existed gain that key with its empty default on load, deterministically and without changing the file on disk. Saving to a bare filename now works in the current directory. Added regression tests: basename round trip, interrupted save, contended save, sequential saves retaining both attempts, wrong-shape rejection, and deterministic migration.
- Delayed quiz outcomes could be recorded before the quiz came due (`src/grindbandit/state.py`, `src/grindbandit/cli.py`). A result stored minutes after scheduling never measured retention after the 3-day delay, yet it sat in the store indistinguishable from a real delayed outcome. `record_delayed_outcome` now rejects a pre-due outcome by default, and the `quiz` command refuses with the due date; `grind-bandit quiz <id> solved|failed --early` is the explicit override, and the outcome is marked early. Outcomes remain observational: recording one still changes mastery, the bandit posterior, problem records, recommendations, and the attempt log by exactly zero.
- Delayed quiz problems repeated while unused problems remained (`src/grindbandit/state.py`). The chooser only avoided problems with a recorded attempt, so the same problem could be handed out as a quiz again and again while never-attempted problems on the topic sat unused. Selection is now deterministic by tier: novel problems first (never attempted, never assigned), then assigned-but-unattempted, then attempted, lowest id inside a tier.
- Recommendation explanations now match the actual sampled draw (`src/grindbandit/bandit.py`). The old explanation derived its driver claim from the posterior mean while selection used a sampled theta, so it could credit a factor that did not drive the realized draw. The logged recommendation record now carries a sampled explanation: per-feature sampled contributions that sum with the intercept to the logged sampled score, the driver equal to the largest sampled contribution, and score uncertainty from 10,000 posterior resamples with the seed recorded. Picks made while the posterior score spread is still wide are labeled exploration, and uncertainty is reported as a model estimate, never as observed proficiency. The `next` command prints the logged explanation lines, and the demo-data helper passes its sampled theta to the explanation instead of recomputing from the mean.
## [v1.2.0] - 2026-10-30
### Docs
- Centered the README header: uniform dark badge labels and three action buttons in the dashboard palette (Open Dashboard, Explore Method, Run Locally).

### Added
- Recommendation logging (`src/grindbandit/bandit.py`, `state.py`, `cli.py`). Every `next` pick now appends a self-contained record to the state file: feature version (`context-v1`), the RNG seed used for the Thompson draw, the posterior and per-topic snapshot, and the full candidate set with context vectors and Monte Carlo selection probability estimates (10,000 posterior resamples on a separate RNG stream, add-one smoothed). `bandit.replay_selection` reconstructs the pick from the record alone, and tests pin that the replay matches the logged selection, before and after a JSON round trip.
- Attempt entries link back to the recommendation that selected the problem (`recommendation_id`) and label the reward as model-derived (`reward_kind: model_derived_mastery_change`); the `log` output uses the same wording.
- Delayed transfer quizzes. Logging an attempt schedules a quiz on the attempt's weakest topic with a different problem, due in 3 days. The new `quiz` command records the outcome into a separate `delayed_quizzes` store; outcomes never change mastery, the bandit posterior, or rewards, and `stats` lists pending quizzes. State files written before this change keep working.

## [v1.1.0] - 2026-10-18
### Fixed
- Forgetting no longer revives on update (`src/grindbandit/mastery.py`). `update` incremented raw counts and reset the decay clock, so after ten successes and 90 idle days (mastery 0.6923) a single failure raised modeled mastery to 0.8462 by resurrecting all the decayed successes. `update` now persists the decayed effective counts at the update time *before* adding the new observation; the raw attempt history stays intact in the append-only log. `replay` accepts the configured half-life so the weekly review reconstructs with the same decay as live mastery. Added regression tests: a failure after decay cannot raise the posterior mean, stored counts are decayed before the new observation lands, and incremental updates agree with chronological log replay across gaps and half-lives.
